Killer 1 · the label is only there for survivors

annual_spend_$ is blank on every lost deal — nobody spends money on a deal that never closed.

So the spend model trains on won deals only. That is survivorship (LSN-0.2), and it is the first thing you say out loud, not the thing they find out in month three.

Killer 2 · the feature that arrives too late

Feed annual_spend_$ into the closed? model and it scores ~100% in the demo.

It is leakage: recorded after the outcome, so at prediction time the column is empty. Ask for the column's timestamp, not its name.

The VP of Sales asks you: "0.73 — so this deal is basically won, right? Should we stop working it?" 12 min. Answers are NOT on the screen — they are in the notebook's key cell and here, for you. Run it as a role-play, in character, and push: "so we can stop working it?" · "so it's 73% accurate?" · "our other vendor's model says 0.9 on the same deal." Make them hold the distinction under pressure; that is what ASM-1 grades. Read two or three pre-work sentences back first, anonymously. Most say "a 73% chance" and stop — which is nearly right and still gets you in trouble, because the missing half is the half that stops the VP cancelling the follow-up call. MODEL ANSWER, part 1 — what it is: 0.73 is the model's estimated probability that this deal closes, given the features it saw. If the model is well calibrated, of 100 deals scored around 0.73, roughly 73 close — and 27 don't. It is a betting line, not a verdict on this deal. MODEL ANSWER, part 2 — what it's for: ranking and prioritizing — a 0.73 deserves attention before a 0.31. It says nothing about why, and it never says "stop working it." (And #4108 in the notebook's pipeline scores 0.31, so the comparison is on screen.) MODEL ANSWER, part 3 — the next questions to ask: Is the score calibrated — when it said 0.7 historically, did ~70% actually close? What features feed it, and how fresh are they? What threshold and action has the client attached to the score? CURVEBALL 1 — the plan's answer, verbatim: "Another deal says 0.51." — near a coin flip; the model is telling you it doesn't know; don't dress it up. Grade the answer that sends it back to human judgement rather than reporting it as "leaning positive". CURVEBALL 2 — the plan's answer, verbatim: "Our forecasting model says 8.2." — that's a regression output; 8.2 what? Units first, interpretation second — a number without units is not an answer. Deals, weeks, percent, millions and out-of-ten are five different meetings. Grade out loud in three verdicts, not right/wrong: MATCH (both halves), DEFENSIBLE (right noun, stopped one sentence early — the commonest real outcome), MISS (the noun is missing, so the client keeps their original reading). The notebook grades the same way and saves it.
