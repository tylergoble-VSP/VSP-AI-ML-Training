MAE, the whole definition

Take every prediction, subtract the truth, throw away the minus sign, average what's left. It comes out in the client's units — which is why you can say it out loud and they can price it.

Segment 2 · Two kinds of wrong, two price tags

Same numerator. Two denominators.
Two different people at the client.

precision  "Can I trust an alert?"

TP / everything we flagged

Falls when there are many false positives. It is the metric of whoever has to work the queue — the analyst, the inspector, the CS rep.

A false positive is an analyst opening a file on a real customer's real purchase. There are 9,999 of those per million.

recall  "How much are we missing?"

TP / everything that was real

Falls when there are many false negatives. It is the metric of whoever eats the loss — the CFO, the warranty budget, the delivery margin.

A false negative is fraud paid out that nobody ever finds out about. It never appears in a queue, so it never gets escalated.

The rule that does all the work

Look at what happens after the model speaks. Cheap and reversible when it's wrong — a review, an inspection, a phone call → push recall. Expensive or irreversible — a declined payment, a cancelled order, an accusation, an automated action nobody sees → push precision.

Which is why "is the model good?" has no answer yet: the priority metric is a property of the ACTION, not of the model. Change what happens when it says yes and the right metric flips — with nothing changed about the model at all.

Segment 4 · 20 min · AUC in one picture

AUC 0.9995 — and the alert queue is still 99% false alarms.

What AUC is, with no maths

A ROC curve is LSN-0.3's threshold sweep, drawn: how much real stuff you catch, against how much noise you take on to get it. AUC is the area under it.

Pick one real fraud and one legitimate transaction at random. How often does the model score the fraud higher? That is AUC. 0.5 is a coin flip; 1.0 is perfect separation.

Two true things at once

the fraud detectorAUC 0.9995, precision 0.98% — would need a 10,098-item review queue funded before it ships
homework card C's churn modelAUC 0.88 — a much worse number, and it is the one you ship

What AUC is blind to

Blind to…Which is why…
the base rateour detector scores 0.9995 and hands over a queue that is 99.02% false alarms. AUC never divides by the alert queue
the thresholdit averages over every threshold, including ones nobody would ship. The client lives at exactly one point on that curve
costit weights a false positive and a false negative identically. No client does
whether the data was faira model graded on rows it trained on can score 0.99 and be worthless. Only the split can see that

Good for choosing between models. Useless for deciding whether to ship one.

So ask for the operating point: "show me precision and recall at the threshold you're actually going to run." AUC picks the model; the operating point decides whether the client can afford it.

The close · the artifact to keep · and next session

"99% accurate" is not a claim you can evaluate.
It is a prompt for four questions.

1 · What's the base rate?

On our detector: 1 in 10,000, so predicting "not fraud" about everything scores 99.99% — the 99% headline is below the do-nothing baseline.

2 · Accuracy on what data?

Did the model see it in training? Ask for the split and the dates. What you want to hear is a holdout from a later period.

3 · Precision and recall, per class?

A model can be excellent on the majority class and useless on the one they're paying for. Two numbers, both true, and the gap is the conversation.

4 · What does each error cost you?
